# ambient-desktop

The Linux notes window. The title is Aerodash. Iced draws the controls. There is no webview. The shell is notes and conversations. A note is one editor. Markdown is styled as it is typed: headings, bold, italic, bold-italic, strike, highlight, code, quotes, lists, tasks, tables, links, images, and note links. Opening `**` starts bold and the closing pair ends it. That word leaves the marks immediately. The run the caret is in, or the link it is in, shows whole with its marks, and so does one the caret was moved to just after. An unclosed mark continues onto the next line until a blank line. The icon at the top right of a note turns on focus, which is for writing with nothing else in view: both panels are away, the title is small at the top right with a button beside it that brings the formatting bar in, the tags are away, and both side panels ease away. Leaving focus brings them back the same way. The panels stay in the row at a width of zero so the page keeps its place in the widget tree and keeps the caret. Focus is only for the wide layout and a note being written. It does not change what the panels remember, so leaving puts everything back. A light on the far right of focus leaves the line under the caret as it is and draws the other lines quieter, and leaving focus turns it off. The writing sits in the middle column, centered, and wraps at a readable measure. Closing the list or the group panel gives that space to the middle. The writing does not run under either panel. A heading on the page is a real size, smaller than the note title, and the same color as the text. A strike is a line through the letters. The marks stay in the text, and that text is what is saved. Enter in the title moves to the writing. Ctrl or Cmd with a full stop is focus. Ctrl or Cmd with Shift and D adds the word at the caret to a personal dictionary, a plain file named `dictionary` in the notebook folder that stays on this computer and is read by the spellcheck. Erasing the notebook erases it and clears the words in memory, so they cannot be written into the next notebook. The Keys card in settings lists the keys. Back up, in settings, copies the notebook folder to a typed place, by hand. The copy holds the key file, so the card warns when the key file is not wrapped. It leaves out `export/`, which is readable Markdown, and the search index. It is made in a `.partial` folder and renamed when whole, never overwrites an earlier copy, refuses a place inside the notebook before making anything, and holds the notes and chat locks while it runs. Do not add a folder-picker crate for it. Do not put anything readable into a copy that the notebook does not already keep sealed. The task field takes the cursor when the Tasks tab opens and keeps it after a task is added. Enter in a list, a task, or a quote inserts the next marker, and Enter on an empty one ends the list, or steps a nested one out a level. Tab nests the list items under the caret or a selection and Shift+Tab brings them out. Ctrl or Cmd with B, I, K, or E is bold, italic, link, or code, and with Shift and X or H is strike or highlight. A new task is unchecked. Backspace on an empty item leaves the list. Backspace at the very start of an item's words takes the item out one level, or at the top level takes the marker off and leaves the words. An ordered list is counted again, from the number its first item has, when Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, or Backspace changes it (`renumbered`), and a list nested under another starts at one. Enter in the middle of an item carries what follows to the next item without the spaces between. A mark key does nothing inside code, in a fence or a backtick span, and the address of a link is left alone. A web address is one word for a mark. Ctrl or Cmd with Enter checks the task the caret is on, or clears it, or makes the line a task. A backtick typed alone comes in a pair with the caret between, typing the closing one steps over it, and Backspace between an empty pair takes both. A `*`, `_`, `~`, or backtick typed on a selection wraps it. Only those four characters look at the page when typed, so an ordinary letter costs nothing. The open note does not keep a second task editor. A task is the toolbar button, or a checkbox line, and the Tasks screen is the view across notes. The panel on the left of the Tasks tab chooses how to look at them (`TaskView`): Views (All open, Due today, Overdue, Upcoming, No date, Done), Groups (the groups the notes with tasks are in, each with its open count, a task belonging to its note's groups), and Notes (each note with a task and its open count). Each shows a count, and a click filters the list on the right. A due moment is the end of a task's line, in one order: a day `YYYY-MM-DD`, then optionally a time `HH:MM` on a 24 hour clock, then optionally a repeat, such as `call the clinic 2026-11-20 14:30 repeat:weekly`. The repeats are `repeat:daily`, `weekdays`, `weekly`, `monthly`, `yearly`, and an interval of two to 99: `repeat:3d`, `2w`, `2m`, `2y`. A repeat with no day is not read, and stays in the words. `due.rs` reads and writes it (`Due::split`, `Due::write`) and is the only code that does. It is only ever written by the calendar (`calendar_card`), opened by the Due date button beside the add field and on a task being edited: a month grid with Today, Tomorrow, Next week, and No date, a time (steps, or 09:00, 12:00, 17:00), and a repeat (Never to Yearly, and every n). There is no field to type a date into. A date that is not a real day, found in a note by hand, counts as no date. A time is the wall clock where the person is, with no zone stored, so a task due at 09:00 is due at 09:00 wherever they are. A task is late once its minute is past, or its day is over if it has no time. Checking a task that repeats does not check it: it moves the day to the next one after the due day and after today, keeps the time and repeat, leaves it open, and says so. There is no record that it was done. `dates.rs` is the pure calendar code. What day and time it is comes from one reading, `App::clock`, which `refresh_clock` takes when the Tasks tab opens, when the window is brought back, before anything that writes a day, and every 20 seconds while the Tasks tab is up, and nothing else looks at a clock, so every date and every late task on a screen agrees. The top of the Tasks panel shows the day, the time, and the zone. Settings has a Time section: the clock reads 12 hour (`3:42 PM`) by default or 24 hour, the calendar's week begins on Sunday by default or Monday, and it says what zone this computer is in and where home is. The choice is `clock::set_hour12`, one answer for every thread, and is kept in the `profile` file. It changes only how a time is shown: what is written on a task is always `HH:MM`. The calendar is a card up to 400 pixels wide with every row spread across its width. On a window wide enough for the panels it floats directly under the Due date button (`anchored`), right edges together, over everything and moving nothing, and flips above the button if there is no room below. On a narrow window (under 720 pixels) it is the standard centered card over a dimmed page (`modal`). A press outside either closes it. **Trips:** the first named zone the folder is opened in is taken as home and kept (`home_zone` in `profile`). A computer that is later in a zone with a different name that sets the clock differently over the next year (`Zone::same_as`, so `US/Pacific` for `America/Los_Angeles` is not a trip) gets a notice on the Tasks tab with both zones and what the clock at home says (`clock::away_from_home`, `trip_banner`), once for each zone, with Hide this and Make this home. It changes nothing on any task: a time is the clock where the person is. The zone comes from the host (`Host::time_zone`) and is read by `zone.rs`, which reads the zone file's list of changes and also the rule in its last line, so the offset is right for summer and for years after the list ends. A test fixes the clock (`pinned_clock`). There are no buttons under the note on a wide window. New note is at the bottom of the left list. Delete shows on a row when the pointer is over it, and asks first. Send to a chat and Delete are in the note's settings, the panel the gear at the top of the right panel switches to, and which also shows the home device. The gear switches back to links and backlinks, the default. A narrow window has no round buttons either: its list has New note at the bottom, and a note has New and a gear in its header, which opens the same panel with Send and Delete. Selecting words shows Send in the pill. It sends only those words, quoted, with the note's title, the lines, and the writing device, and cites the note. The Tasks note is not in the notes list. The Tasks tab opens it in a card. The tag field offers tags used in other notes as you type, those that start with the text first. A wheel notch moves a page step, as GTK does, not a fixed 60 pixels, and a touchpad is left as the system sends it. A key the editor did not take, while the page is up, is typed into the page from the window listener, so fast typing keeps every letter. Delete still asks, then writes a snapshot whose body is only the delete marker, and the list hides that note. A tag is a chip under the title, stored on a hidden line at the end of the note, and never read from a `#` in the writing. The left list files notes by those chips. A `#` in the writing is ordinary text. A pause saves the open note on this computer even when the relay is off. That write runs off the typing thread and does not reload the editor. Scrollbars are not drawn. A faint thin thumb shows for under a second after the pane under the pointer scrolls, and a clear one while the pointer is on the bar, so it can still be dragged. A format row lives in the note column, above the editor, when the window is wide, and at the bottom when the window is narrower than 720. It does not sit over the list. The buttons are grouped as Word, Line, and Insert, and each one has a hover label. A word mark (bold, italic, strike, highlight, code) wraps the selection, or the word under the caret, and toggles off if that word is already wrapped. A line mark (heading, list, task, quote) toggles the lines the caret touches. An insert (link, note link, image, table, rule, callout, code block) drops that block. After a drag, a range that is more than a caret shows a pill above those letters: bold, italic, strike, highlight, link, copy, cut, and paste. Escape clears the range. The line marks and the block inserts stay on the bar. Links do not make groups or chapters. A note with many links is an ordinary note. Under the tags, `2 links · 1 linked from` opens the right panel on them. **Read** belongs to a group: with a group in view in the left list, Read draws that group's notes, those tagged with it or inside it, one after another in title order, with a Back to writing button. No combined note is written. The old rule where two links made a contents note, a `Kind:` line, and a chapter rail is removed. The count and that list wait out a short pause, so a keystroke only moves the caret and the letters. A note link stores the note's id and shows its title. An older link that is only a title still opens. Typing `[[` offers notes. A link with no note is drawn as unresolved. Command-click opens it. A plain click still places the caret. A missing name creates a note with that title. **Links and groups are different things, on purpose.** A link is a reference. The open note's right panel shows `Links to` (from `SideDone::links_to`) and `Linked from` (backlinks). A link never puts a note inside another in the left list. A **group** is a tag: `Novel` is a group, and `Novel/Part 1` is a group inside it. A tag is stored on the note's hidden tag line, so a group needs no record, no reference note, and no merge rule of its own. The word group in the notes UI has nothing to do with a multi-member Space, which is what the other docs mean by a group. Notes in a group are alphabetical, the way a person counts (`natural_cmp`, so Chapter 2 comes before Chapter 10), until the owner moves one. Moving is by hand: hover a note in a group, take the grip at its right, and drop it on another note in the same group to put it beside that note, or on the group's name to put it first. Only a note tagged with exactly that group can be moved there. The result is `orders`, a group key to note ids, written to the plain file `order` in the notebook folder by `profile::store_order`. A note not listed comes after the listed ones, alphabetically, so a new note never disappears. **This order is on this computer only and does not sync**, and Read follows it. `order_ids` and `move_in_order` are the pure parts. `group_outline` builds the left list from the tags: groups first, folded with a handle, each with a count of the notes in it and in the groups inside it, then notes with no tag. A note with two tags shows in both groups. Case and spacing around a slash do not make a second group (`clean_tag`, `group_key`). Clicking a group's name shows only that group's notes, and a row above them clears it. The graph, behind the button at the top of the left panel, takes the place of the note. Each top-level group (the first part of a note's first tag) is a cluster of dots inside a large soft circle in the group's color, with its name at the top of the circle, and notes with no group are a muted cluster of their own. A dot is a note, a line is a link, and a line from one group to another is drawn heavier. Titles wrap at a fixed width. The picture is kept clear of the edge by `GRAPH_MARGIN` and sits in a card. Clicking a dot opens that note. `build_graph` lays each group out on its own (`settle_group`), then moves the circles apart, with groups that link most kept nearest (`place_groups`). It is fixed and runs once when the graph opens or the list changes, so the same notes give the same picture. Only the top level of a group path is a circle.  The marks stay in the text. That narrow layout is also the list-then-page layout, so the window can sit beside another program. Chat messages are bubbles, yours on the right, in advisory-time order with the latest at the bottom. A file added in chat is copied into the notebook and cited from the message. The citation can sync. The bytes cannot. A video opens with the system. It is not played in the window. The file chip uses the bubble's text color. A globe at the right of the toolbar is the sync status: gray when nothing is connected, green when a relay is up and caught up, red when a relay is up and something is unsent. On a narrow window the globe is on the bottom bar. A checkmark beside it means the open note is saved on this computer. Advanced, behind the globe, is where a pinned relay or a backup is typed. The default is this computer. A gear opens settings. A sun or moon sets an explicit light or dark. Settings can also follow the system. Those choices, your name, and what you call someone are local files, not records. The contact card still has no name. Clicking the globe opens a popover, with the detail and the relay switch. The first screen is the word Aerodash, one letter at a time, over the two lines, for at least three seconds and until the folder is open. A new folder asks for a device name, a local personal name, and a color. An existing key skips that. The relay starts when the folder opens. The caret's thread paints and takes keys. Two background threads touch the folder, and a third only checks spelling. It never takes a lock. Notes and conversations use different locks. A full sync takes the notes lock first, then the chat lock, so a save cannot land in the middle of a sync. While a conversation is on screen, one thread pulls that mailbox a few times a second and appends new lines. A just-sent line is written locally and pushed immediately. The other thread builds the group list, the word counts, and the notes that link here, and it runs the slower full sync of notes and devices. A note search does not block a chat pull. The full sync leaves the open conversation to the faster pull. The group list is ready when the folder opens, before a note is clicked. A click reads the projection already in memory. The window keeps what it drew until a result arrives. A short pause writes the open note without moving the cursor. That pause is one wake, not a timer that redraws the window. Invite is a screen. Devices, recovery, people, and erasing the folder are settings. A device list can revoke a key from the root folder. The note list shows which device wrote a note. The page does not. The writer is in the note's settings. An unsaved note stays when you leave it. List rows are not the same control as Save, Send, or New note. After a pause, a misspelled word is underlined and a mark that is still open is one quiet line. The word list and where it came from are in `dict/`. Add a missing word to `dict/words-extra.txt`, not to the generated `dict/words.txt`. Opening a note checks that at once. Search shows a title match at once. The matching line under a note, and a conversation when a chat line matches, wait a moment and only the latest query runs. Conversation text is reread only when a log changes. A note link can name one version, written `[[id@version|title]]`. An older version is one event from an index built when the log was loaded. It opens read-only and is not saved over the current note. A task line and a chat line can cite a note. The citation is the note id. It is not a grant.

It calls [`Notebook`](../ambient-cli/src/session.rs) and the sync helpers in `ambient-cli`. The folder is the same one the CLI uses.

## Invariants

- Do not put protocol rules in this crate. Sealing, event encoding, and the relay JSON stay in the crates that already own them.
- The only network is the loopback relay that client starts or dials. Cleartext on `127.0.0.1` is not a public deployment. The window must say so.
- Do not claim the key file is the OS keystore, or that a paired device holds the root.
- This is the Linux app. Do not turn it into the Mac or Windows shell.

## Dependencies

May depend on: `ambient-cli`. The window is a client, not a protocol crate, so it is excluded from `just wasm-check`.

## Test

Editor decisions are tested in `ambient-cli` (`Draft`). Sync and pairing are tested there too. This crate is the window.

```
cargo run -p ambient-desktop -- path/to/folder
```

With no path it uses `./notebook`.

## Find and replace, and a note's date

Ctrl or Cmd with F finds in the open note, and Ctrl or Cmd with H, or the Replace button, shows a Replace with field and Replace and Replace all. Replace changes the match the bar is on, or the first one after the caret, and goes to the next. Replace all is one change from the first match to the last, so one undo takes it all back. Matching ignores ASCII case, and the replacement is written as typed. The notes list can be limited to those changed in the past day, week, month, or year, from the chips under the search field. A note's date, `NoteSummary::modified`, is the newest advisory time of its snapshots. It is built as the log loads and kept a write at a time (`Projected::modified`), so a list does not parse snapshots to show it. **A note event now carries the advisory time field**, as a conversation line always did, so a note written before that has no date and is left out when a time is asked for. The filter is cleared when the search is closed.

## Recently deleted and version history

A delete is a snapshot whose body is only the marker, so what a note said before is still in the log. `Notebook::deleted_held` lists the notes that were deleted, newest first, each as it read just before (the last snapshot reachable from the delete through its parents that has words in it). The trash button at the top of the notes list opens them in place of the note, each with Bring back. `Notebook::versions_held` lists every snapshot a note has had, newest first, with when, who, and whether it is a current head. The note's panel has Version history, which lists them with Read and Restore. Read is the older page, which is not the note and is not saved, and which also has Restore this version. **Restoring is an edit:** it writes the old words as a new snapshot whose parents are the current heads, so nothing in the log changes, and the restore can itself be undone by restoring a later version. A restore is refused while the open note has typing that is not saved yet, so those words are not written over. Both views are the same list-then-detail on a narrow window.

## Popups, panels, and narrow windows

Nothing that opens may push the page. There are three ways to open something, and a new thing uses one of them.

- **A menu or card tied to a button** is `anchored(button, card, dismiss)`: it floats directly under the button, clamped to the window with eight pixels to spare, above the button if there is no room below, and a press anywhere else sends `dismiss`. The sync card, the Cite menus (tasks and chat), and the calendar on a wide window use it. Give the card a width of its own, or `Fill` with a `max_width`, never a bare `Fill` that would be the window's. **It is an overlay, and an overlay only reaches the window if every widget between it and the window passes it up.** `Wheel` did not, from the commit that made the wheel scroll a page step until the one that added `overlay` to it, and for that time no menu under a button and no hover label was drawn. A widget of ours that wraps a child must forward `overlay`, and `operate`, and `mouse_interaction`.
- **A question or a choice** is a card in `modal()`, centered over a dimmed page, the same at any width: delete, send to a chat, clear done tasks, and, on a narrow window, the calendar. A press on the dimmed page closes it.
- **The panel on the right** is beside the page from 1020 pixels wide, and a sheet from the right edge over a dimmed page below that and on a narrow window (`context_sheet`), with a Close button. Narrowing the window past that point closes it rather than putting it over the writing.

Both of the last two are one `dim_layer`: a single layer holding the dim part and the content, so that a press on the dim part cannot be taken by a layer above it. (A stack hides the layers under one whose pointer is over something that wants it.) `view` stacks them over `screen_view`, which is always the first layer, so opening one never moves the screen in the tree and never costs a field its caret. A field's own container must also keep its shape (see the tag field). The suggestions that appear while typing `[[` in a note (`link_menu`) are still in the flow of the page and still move it.

**Narrow windows** (under 720 pixels) are one model for every tab: the list, then the thing chosen from it, with a way back at the top of the second (`detail_header`, `BackToList`). Notes: the list of notes, then a note. Chat: the conversations, then one. Tasks: the ways to look at the tasks (what the panel on the left is on a wide window), then the tasks in the one chosen. The graph is opened from the list and goes back to it. Pressing a tab's name in the bottom bar always lands on that tab's list. A row of buttons that could run off the edge wraps (`Row::wrap`), and the add row of the Tasks tab puts its field on one line and its buttons on the next when the pane is under 560 pixels. In a conversation, Add, Cite, and Send are icons on one line with the box at every width.

`headless.rs` lays out, draws, and clicks the whole window on the software renderer, with the program's fonts, at the widths 1400, 1100, 1019, 900, 760, 719, 600, 420, and 340. Use it for anything about where things are or what a press does: a menu that is drawn, a press on the dimmed page that closes a card, a box that runs past the right edge of the window (a scroll area's content may). It rasterizes nothing, so it cannot say how anything looks. Look at the window for that. A new popup or screen belongs in its sweep. The older `every_screen_draws_at_every_width_with_every_popup_open` only builds views and is the weaker check.
